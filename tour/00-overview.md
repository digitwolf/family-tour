# Trip Overview

A scenic, family-friendly **8-day Pacific Northwest motorcycle coast tour** for a family of three — built to **grow a newer rider's skills and her love of touring**, not to chase mileage. It runs as a relaxed grand loop from home — around the Olympic Peninsula, down the whole northern Oregon coast, and back through Portland — on the family's **own** bikes, entirely on paved two-lanes off the freeways.

## The goal
This is the follow-up to Galiya's first tour: a **coast-and-glamping loop** with two safari-style camps, two genuinely nice hotels, and two full rest days. Everything — the route, the daily distances, the road choices, the fuel stops — is shaped to keep the riding comfortable and joyful for a still-new rider, with wild beaches for Aslan and the coast's best food for Galiya.

## The family
Full profiles, riding backgrounds and a "what we like" planning checklist are in **[00-family.md](00-family.md)** — read it before planning.
- **Rider 2 — Galiya** — the **newly-endorsed solo rider** this whole trip is built around, on her own **Kawasaki W230** (233cc single, happiest 55–65 mph, ~3.4-gal tank). She rides up front; **her confidence sets the pace**. A big foodie who loves the coast, seafood, lighthouses and art.
- **Rider 1 — Ruslan (37)** — seasoned rider (BMW R1300GS; a 2-week Morocco ADV trip), carrying **Aslan as pillion** plus the snacks, rain layers, tools and first-aid. He is *not* the limiting rider — he rides steady and keeps Galiya's pace.
- **Passenger — Aslan (6)** — rides with dad in full fitting gear, with stops every 60–90 minutes. Can manage up to ~6h on a good day but **not long days back-to-back**. Loves toys/souvenirs, animals, hands-on museums, tents that feel like expeditions, and the cheese factory.

## Shape of the trip
- **A grand loop from Woodinville, WA** (15402 NE 176th Pl, 98072) — **out around the Olympic Peninsula and down the Oregon coast, home through the Willamette Valley and Portland.** The family rides their **own** motorcycles round-trip: **no rental, no flights, no passport or IDP.** The trip starts in the garage (see [getting started](02-getting-started.md)).
- **Dates:** depart **Saturday 15 August 2026**, home **Saturday 22 August 2026** — **8 days, 7 nights**. Two rest days: **Tue Aug 18** at the Two Capes Lookout glamping base and **Thu Aug 20** in Yachats.
- **One ferry, no freeways:** Day 1 opens by rolling the bikes onto the short **Edmonds–Kingston ferry** (Washington State Ferries — no motorcycle reservation; arrive early) to skip Seattle traffic, then crosses the Hood Canal Bridge onto the Olympic Peninsula. From there the route stays on **coastal and valley two-lanes the whole way** — deliberately **off all Interstates and high-speed highways** for the new rider.
- **≈ 945 riding miles over 8 days.** Distances and drive times are from the **Google Directions API** (real road routing). The **average riding day is ≈ 2¾h**; five of the eight days are **under 2¾ hours**. The one long day — **Day 2** (Forks → Astoria, ~200 mi) — follows the easy ferry day, with breaks every 60–90 minutes for Aslan. **Day 8 is the one planned freeway leg:** a short ~3h I-5 run home so the trip ends early and easy.
- **Two 2-night bases:** the **Two Capes Lookout** glamping domes at Tierra Del Mar (Days 3–4, between Cape Lookout and Cape Kiwanda) and oceanfront **Yachats** (Days 5–6, the "nice hotel" splurge). Single-night stops at a **Forks safari tent** (Day 1), Astoria's **Cannery Pier Hotel & Spa** on stilts over the Columbia (Day 2), and a **Portland** downtown hotel (Day 7).
- **The lodging is half the adventure:** safari tent → historic-pier hotel → geodesic dome → oceanfront lodge → city hotel — every night is its own event for a six-year-old.
- **Best timing:** mid-August is the Pacific Northwest's driest, warmest window — the marine fog burns off by late morning, resident gray whales feed off Depoe Bay, and Portland's rose garden is in late-summer bloom. The trade-off is peak season on the coast: book every night well ahead.

## Avoid
Freeways and Interstates (except the one planned Day-8 I-5 run home, ridden steady in the right lane), high-speed highways, long back-to-back transfers, riding after dark, pushed-hard wet twisties, gambling on the W230's fuel range, and Seattle traffic (the ferry exists to skip it).

## Child-pillion safety (BMW R1300GS)
The 6-year-old rides on the GS with Ruslan. Set up and test all of this **while the bike is parked** before Day 1, then confirm it on the gentle ferry-and-peninsula opener before the longer days:

- **Footpeg reach.** A 6-year-old's legs may not reach the stock passenger pegs — fit **lowered/highway pegs or peg-lowering brackets** so feet sit flat and secure; dangling feet get tired and slip.
- **Backrest & grab points.** Add a **passenger backrest** so he can't slide rearward, plus a **proper grab rail or grab strap** at the waist — don't rely on him holding the rider.
- **Tandem riding belt.** Use a **child–adult tandem safety belt** so a tired child can't lean away or doze off the bike.
- **Gear.** A properly-fitted child helmet (correct shell size, not an adult hand-me-down), armoured jacket, gloves, boots; ear protection helps on longer legs.
- **Pace & limits.** Stops every **60–90 minutes**; watch for him going quiet or fidgety (early fatigue). Keep the long Day-2 transfer steady and broken into short hops, and **never run two long days back-to-back** — the Day-8 freeway run home is short (~3h) and split by a Castle Rock lunch.

## Before you go (per rider)
Valid Washington/Oregon **motorcycle endorsement** (Galiya's) · current registration & insurance on both bikes · pre-trip service (tires, chain/final drive, brakes, lights) · **intercoms paired** · confirmed **secure motorcycle parking + passenger/child rules** at each lodging (and the guest count/child fit at both glamping camps) · offline maps downloaded for the Olympic coast. No passport, IDP or rental paperwork — these are the family's own bikes (see [getting started](02-getting-started.md)).
